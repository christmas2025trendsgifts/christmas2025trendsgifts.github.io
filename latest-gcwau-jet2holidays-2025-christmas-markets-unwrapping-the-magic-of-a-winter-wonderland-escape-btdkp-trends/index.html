A Kaleidoscope of Festive Destinations for 2025

While specific itineraries for 2025 will be unveiled closer to the booking period, based on their historical offerings and popular demand, Jet2holidays is expected to feature an impressive array of European cities, each promising a unique festive flavour. Let’s explore some of the perennial favourites that are likely to form the core of the jet2holidays 2025 christmas markets portfolio:

1. Cologne, Germany: A Cathedral of Lights and Cheer
Cologne is often considered the quintessential German Christmas market destination, and for good reason. Dominated by the magnificent Cologne Cathedral, a UNESCO World Heritage site, the city hosts several distinct markets, each with its own charm. The most famous is the ‘Weihnachtsmarkt am Kölner Dom,’ nestled directly beneath the soaring spires of the cathedral. Here, hundreds of red-roofed stalls offer traditional crafts, glühwein served in commemorative mugs, and an overwhelming sense of festive joy. Other notable markets include the charming ‘Heinzels Wintermärchen’ (Home of the Heinzelmännchen, the house gnomes) in the Old Town, known for its ice rink and rustic appeal, and the ‘Harbour Christmas Market’ by the Chocolate Museum, with its maritime theme. Cologne’s markets are a symphony of light, sound, and scent, promising an unforgettable experience.

2. Prague, Czech Republic: A Fairytale Frozen in Time
Prague, with its Gothic architecture and cobblestone streets, transforms into a breathtaking winter wonderland during the Christmas season. The main markets at Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square are the epicentre of the festivities. The Old Town Square market, in particular, is a visual spectacle, with a Walt disney world very merry christmas a guide to the most magical holiday celebration towering Christmas tree adorned with thousands of lights, a live nativity scene, and traditional wooden stalls selling Bohemian crystal, wooden toys, and delectable trdelník (a sweet pastry cooked over an open flame). The medieval setting adds an extra layer of enchantment, making it feel as though you’ve stepped directly into a classic Christmas story. The aroma of roasted ham and hot mead fills the air, inviting visitors to linger and soak in the festive atmosphere.

3. Vienna, Austria: Imperial Grandeur Meets Festive Charm
Vienna’s Christmas markets are renowned for their elegance and imperial backdrop. The ‘Wiener Christkindlmarkt’ on Rathausplatz (City Hall Square) is arguably the most famous, transforming the square into a glittering fairytale land. With the neo-Gothic City Hall as its stunning backdrop, this market features a vast array of stalls, a large ice rink, and a magical "Herzerlbaum" (tree of hearts). Other exquisite markets include the one in front of Schönbrunn Palace, offering a regal setting and a focus on traditional Austrian crafts, and the market at Belvedere Palace, known for its Baroque splendour. Vienna’s markets offer a sophisticated festive experience, complete with classical music performances and gourmet treats.

4. Budapest, Hungary: Danube Dreams and Festive Delights
Budapest, straddling the majestic Danube, offers a unique blend of historical grandeur and vibrant modern culture, especially during the Christmas season. The two main markets, Vörösmarty Square and the Advent Feast at St. Stephen’s Basilica, are must-visits. Vörösmarty Square is the oldest and most traditional, offering authentic Hungarian crafts, kürtőskalács (chimney cake), and spicy mulled wine. The Advent Feast at St. Stephen’s Basilica is perhaps even more spectacular, featuring a stunning light show projected onto the basilica’s façade, an ice rink, and a more contemporary feel. Budapest’s markets are known for their lively atmosphere, delicious street food, and unique Hungarian gifts, making for a truly memorable festive escape.

5. Krakow, Poland: Medieval Magic in the Main Square
Krakow’s Christmas market, held in the vast and beautiful Rynek Główny (Main Market Square), is one of Europe’s most atmospheric. Surrounded by historic buildings, including the Cloth Hall and St. Mary’s Basilica, the market offers a truly authentic Polish festive experience. Stalls overflow with traditional Polish crafts, amber jewellery, sheepskin products, and delicious local delicacies like oscypek (smoked cheese from the Tatra mountains), pierogi, and grzane wino (mulled wine). The market is a hub of activity, with carol singers, folk dancers, and the occasional horse-drawn carriage adding to the enchanting ambiance. Krakow provides a charming, less commercialised alternative, steeped in history and genuine warmth.
